# Chapter 11: Use Case Formats

## Core Idea

Choose a format that is readable and proportionate to risk; Cockburn favors a one-column, numbered, fully dressed form for detailed requirements.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Fully dressed template**: name, context, scope, level, primary actor, stakeholders/interests, precondition, minimal guarantees, success guarantees, trigger, MSS, extensions, variations, and related information.
  - When to use: a high-value, reviewable behavioral contract.
  - How: complete header intent before writing scenarios.
- **Casual use case**: a lighter narrative form for low ceremony.
- **Brief**: a concise description for early discovery, estimation, or agile coordination.

## Worked Example

Use a brief to decide whether “Register arrival” belongs in a release. Promote it to fully dressed only when the team needs exact stakeholder protections, boundary conditions, and extension behavior.

## Key Takeaways

1. Format supports communication; it is not the method itself.
2. One-column numbered steps often maximize readability.
3. Avoid if-statements in the MSS; place the condition in extensions.
