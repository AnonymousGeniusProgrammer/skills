# Chapter 5: Three Named Goal Levels

## Core Idea

Control the level of abstraction: use cases work best when their titles and steps stay at a purposeful, consistent goal altitude.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Three named goal levels**: summary (white), user-goal/sea-level (blue), and subfunction (indigo).
  - When to use: selecting what deserves its own use case.
  - How: use user-goal as the default; use summary for a lifecycle/context and subfunctions only for justified detail.
- **Coffee-break test**: a user-goal lets a primary actor complete meaningful work in one sitting and go away satisfied.
  - When to use: detecting over- or under-granular titles.
- **How/why test**: ask “why?” to find the next higher goal and “how?” to find the next lower goal.

## Key Concepts

- **Summary goal**: spans multiple user goals or long timescales.
- **User-goal**: meaningful work, often 2–20 minutes in one sitting.
- **Subfunction**: supporting goal beneath a user goal.
- **Clam/too low**: behavior that should remain a step rather than become a use case.

## Worked Example

“Operate insurance policy” is a summary because it spans obtaining a quote, buying, claiming, and closing. “Buy policy” can be a user-goal. “Read card” or “press tab” is too low-level to stand alone.

## Key Takeaways

1. Sea-level goals justify the system and guide estimates, releases, and teams.
2. A long, dull requirements set commonly indicates underwater decomposition.
3. Use 3–10 steps as a practical signal that the level may be right.
